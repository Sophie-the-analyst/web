# Descriptive Analysis Report: ABU Uncovered — Students’ Favourite Spots

**Dataset:** Survey responses on students’ favourite spots in and around Ahmadu Bello University (ABU)
**Number of responses:** 36
**Analysis type:** Simple descriptive analysis using counts and percentages

The dataset contains **36 responses** collected on 24 October 2026. It records students’ level of study, faculty, gender, favourite spot, spot type, reasons for liking the spot, visit frequency, companions, average spending, recommendation likelihood, and other suggested locations.

This is a descriptive summary of the respondents, not a representative estimate of all ABU students. Percentages are calculated from the 36 responses unless otherwise stated.

## 5. What students value about their favourite spots

Responses to the open-ended question were varied. Repeated and recurring themes included:

- **Quietness and calm:** students described places as quiet, peaceful, serene, or calm.
- **Relaxation and atmosphere:** respondents valued the general vibe, comfort, spaciousness, and opportunity to unwind.
- **Nature and scenery:** greenery, flowers, fresh air, and breeze were mentioned.
- **Food and drinks:** good food and particular food offerings were reasons for choosing some locations.
- **Social connection:** some respondents valued meeting friends and people, and the social life around the location.
- **Study environment:** quietness and suitability for reading were important for library or study-oriented locations.

These themes are qualitative interpretations of individual responses; a single response may express more than one theme.

## 11. Key findings

1. **DLC Garden was the leading favourite spot**, with 8 of 36 responses (22.2%).
2. **Relaxation and hangout spaces were most common**, followed by food spots and study locations.
3. **Quietness, greenery, comfort, food, and social connection** were recurring reasons students liked their chosen spots.
4. **More than half of respondents visit frequently**: 55.6% visit almost every day or several times a week.
5. Favourite locations are used both socially and individually: friends were the most common companions, while many also visited alone.
6. **Spending was generally reported in the lowest band or as occasion-dependent**; the data do not allow a reliable average-spend estimate.
7. **Recommendation was generally positive**, with 72.2% saying they would definitely recommend their spot.

## 12. Limitations

- The dataset contains only 36 responses, so findings should not be generalized to all ABU students.
- The respondents are concentrated in 500 level and Pharmaceutical Sciences, which may influence the results.
- The survey appears to rely on voluntary responses; selection bias is possible.
- Open-ended responses and location names contain spelling, capitalization, and naming variations.
- Some spot-type answers include multiple selections, so category counts overlap.
- The “average spending” question uses spending bands and an “it depends” option, so a numerical mean cannot be calculated reliably.

## 13. Conclusion

In this sample, students most often named DLC Garden and valued places that provide relaxation, quietness, greenery, food, or opportunities to connect with others. Frequent visits and the high proportion willing to recommend their chosen spots suggest that these locations play a meaningful role in students’ campus life. A larger and more diverse sample would help establish whether the same patterns hold across ABU’s wider student population.
